PHILOSOPHICAL ESSAY
01 Introduction

Life can be interpreted in many ways. Some people see it as a path, others as a sequence of decisions, and others believe it is largely shaped by the circumstances around us.

The Roulette Theory of Life begins from a different perspective: existence can be understood as a collection of countless roulettes that keep spinning from the beginning of our lives onward.

Each roulette represents a category of events, possibilities, or circumstances that can influence our lives. These include the roulettes of success, opportunity, health, death, failure, and many others that need not have a fixed limit.

They do not stop to wait for us, announce when they will affect us, or necessarily respond to our wishes. They may choose us at any moment, have effects of different magnitudes, and keep spinning after they have influenced our existence.

This perspective does not claim to prove that a literal mechanism determines life's events. The roulette is a metaphor for reflecting on uncertainty and our inability to know in advance everything that might happen to us.

The central idea is that we can never fully know the possibilities ahead, yet we can keep living, learning, and pursuing our goals while those possibilities remain open.

02 The concept of the roulettes

In this theory, a roulette is a conceptual representation of a category of events that may occur during a person's life.

Not all roulettes represent the same thing. Some are connected to events that may support our goals; others represent difficult circumstances, unexpected changes, or situations over which we have little control.

The roulette of success represents the possibility of recognition or significant achievement. The roulette of opportunity represents possibilities that may change our path. The roulette of health represents changes in well-being, while the roulette of death represents uncertainty about the end of life.

These categories are examples, not a definitive classification. Countless roulettes may exist, and each can be interpreted according to the event it represents.

A person's life may be influenced by several roulettes whose circumstances and effects intersect in ways we cannot always understand.

03 The constant motion of the roulettes

The roulettes never stop spinning. This is one of the theory's central ideas.

Time keeps moving, and events unfold that can change our circumstances. There is no guarantee that these events will appear when we expect them or in the order we consider convenient.

From the beginning of our existence, we are exposed to circumstances we cannot fully predict. Some may influence us before we are aware of them. Others may appear when we are adults, when we think we have found our path, or when we believe we have already achieved what we wanted.

One person may experience a major change in seconds, while another may spend decades without finding an opportunity they consider transformative.

The continuous motion of the roulettes represents our inability to know when events that might change our existence will occur.

04 The dependence on time

All roulettes depend on time, because the events they represent unfold throughout existence.

Time allows circumstances to change, new possibilities to appear, and past experiences to acquire different meanings. However, time does not guarantee that a particular opportunity will arrive.

Someone may wait years for an event that never happens, while another person may experience an unexpected change at a moment that seemed insignificant.

We can anticipate certain possibilities, prepare for some circumstances, and make long-term plans, but our predictions do not eliminate uncertainty.

05 Randomness and the limits of our influence

Within this perspective, the roulettes are random. This means we cannot know for certain what events will happen to us, when they will occur, or what consequences they will have.

We can try to shape our circumstances through decisions, knowledge, and effort. Yet these attempts do not guarantee that the future will match our expectations.

A person may devote their life to an activity and never achieve the expected result. Another may discover an opportunity in a field they had never considered.

Preparation and effort can influence our possibilities, but they do not give us complete control over outcomes.

Someone may study music hoping to become well-known in the music industry. They can practice for years, improve their skills, and develop their own style, but they cannot guarantee fame.

Along the way, they may discover programming and find possibilities they had never considered: building tools for musicians, creating virtual instruments, or designing software for music production. They might also use those skills for something entirely different.

The theory does not claim that effort is worthless. It claims that effort cannot guarantee a specific result, and that life may present possibilities different from those we expected.

06 The roulettes may choose us without our noticing

An event may happen without us being aware of its potential importance. Not every meaningful event arrives dramatically. Some seem insignificant when they happen and gain a different meaning only after our circumstances change.

Someone may meet a person, learn a skill, or make a decision without understanding how it could relate to their future. Years later, they may discover that the experience helped open a possibility they had not known about. They may also never discover such a connection.

Therefore, we cannot claim that every apparently insignificant event hides an important opportunity. The point is that our perception is limited and we cannot always recognize every possible consequence as events unfold.

When someone believes they have never had an opportunity, it is also possible that they experienced circumstances whose value they did not recognize. This does not mean every opportunity is easy to identify or possible to take.

07 The repetition of the same roulette

A roulette may choose us several times during our lives. But being chosen again does not mean its effects will be the same as before.

A person's circumstances change over time. A similar event can therefore have different consequences depending on when it occurs.

Someone may achieve extraordinary success for the first time and experience a profound transformation. Years later, they may achieve another success of similar scale, but the second event may not change their life as intensely because they already have recognition, experience, and an established career.

The opposite may also happen: a second success may surpass the first and transform their career in an unexpected way.

The repetition of a roulette does not guarantee the repetition of its effects. Every event takes place within different circumstances.

08 The multiplicity of roulettes

There is no single roulette that determines every aspect of our existence. The theory allows for countless categories of events, each related to different dimensions of life.

  • Temporary success: achievements whose impact may be intense but limited in time.
  • Lasting success: achievements that may become established and maintain a lasting influence.
  • Opportunity: unexpected possibilities that may change our path.
  • Health: circumstances related to our well-being.
  • Death: uncertainty about the end of our existence.
  • Learning: knowledge and skills that may affect future possibilities.
  • Relationships: encounters and changes in our relationships with others.
  • Failure: adverse outcomes and circumstances that may prevent certain goals.
  • Life: the continuation of existence and the range of circumstances that may unfold within it.

This list is not exhaustive. One event may relate to several roulettes at once. The theory does not claim that every event belongs exclusively to one category.

09 The value of what we did not expect

We do not always find what we were looking for. Sometimes a person pursues a goal for years without reaching it. Along the way, they may gain knowledge, develop skills, or discover interests that were not part of their original plans.

Someone studying music may later find an opportunity related to programming. Knowledge from one activity may complement knowledge from the other, even if the connection was not obvious at first.

This does not mean every experience will eventually prove useful. It means an experience's value cannot always be judged solely by the goal it was meant to achieve.

Some things do not produce the results we wanted. Others leave us with difficult consequences. Not every event has to become a reward for us to keep living.

10 The decision to try

If we cannot fully control the roulettes, why should we try?

Uncertainty does not erase the value of action. A person can pursue goals without knowing whether they will achieve them. They can learn, work, develop skills, and explore new possibilities without knowing the outcome.

Trying does not mean demanding that life reward our efforts. It means acting despite not knowing the result.

We can set priorities, choose the goals we want to pursue, and decide which opportunities deserve our attention. The theory does not suggest pursuing every roulette at once, but recognizing that our plans cannot cover every possibility in existence.

Trying is a decision we can make. Guaranteeing the result is not.

11 The uncertainty of success and death

Success and death represent very different dimensions of existence, yet both illustrate our inability to know with certainty what lies ahead.

A person may spend their life pursuing success without knowing whether they will reach the result they want. Likewise, no one can know with certainty how much time they have left.

These situations are not equivalent in their consequences or nature. Still, both show the limits of our ability to predict the future.

Uncertainty does not remove the possibility of making meaningful decisions. It reminds us that our plans unfold within a reality whose full outcome we do not know.

12 The limits of the theory

The Roulette Theory of Life is a philosophical interpretation of uncertainty and the events that influence our existence.

It does not claim to prove that a force selects events for each person or that all events follow a mechanism of absolute randomness.

It also does not claim that every experience has a hidden purpose, that suffering necessarily leads to a reward, or that every opportunity can be recognized if we pay enough attention.

Some events cannot be predicted, some circumstances cannot be controlled, and some opportunities may never become known to us. Our decisions can influence the likelihood of certain outcomes without guaranteeing them.

Its scope is philosophical and reflective. Its principles can be revised, expanded, or questioned as new examples and objections are considered.

13 Conclusion

Life can be understood as a collection of countless roulettes that keep spinning. We do not know how many there are, when they will affect us, or what consequences their events will have. Some may deeply influence our existence; others may create small changes or pass unnoticed.

The same roulette may choose us more than once and produce different results. An event may seem insignificant when it happens and gain new meaning years later. We may also never fully understand its consequences.

We cannot control every circumstance or guarantee that our efforts will produce the results we want. Yet we can keep learning, exploring possibilities, and working toward what matters to us.

Uncertainty does not guarantee that we will reach our goals, but it also does not allow us to claim that we already know every path that may become available to us.

This perspective suggests trying without demanding absolute guarantees, recognizing our limits without abandoning our ability to act, and remaining open to possibilities we might never have imagined.

The roulettes do not have to stop when we are ready. They do not have to offer us what we expected. We simply do not know which events will unfold during our lives or how they will affect us.

Perhaps an opportunity will appear when we least expect it. Perhaps an experience will gain a meaning we could not recognize before. Perhaps a different path will become more important than we imagined. Or perhaps some possibilities will never come to pass.

We cannot know every answer in advance.

The roulettes keep spinning. We keep living, making decisions, and trying to understand an existence whose full outcome we can never know.

And as life continues, there will always be events we have not experienced, possibilities we cannot yet imagine, and questions we do not yet know how to answer.
